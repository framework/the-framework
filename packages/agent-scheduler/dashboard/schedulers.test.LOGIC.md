What the tests cover:

- **A project's row from what status printed** - for a scheduler that ticked in a project whose skills schedule two commands, each scheduled command carries this machine's schedule switch and publish pick, what its skill says it does and the last tick's decision for it: a command nobody switched on here reads as off, with the pick of nothing made here and its skill's plain line for what its check waits for; a command switched on here that nobody picked for reads as on, with the pick `commit`; the project's row carries the scheduler's on, keep-alive, running, model and spend cushion, the last tick's time and decisions, and no skill whose `schedule` could not be read. A scheduler that never ticked lists no command and names no such skill. A field that is not what the command promises reads as absent: an `on` that is a word, a cushion that is a word, a model that is a number, a switch that is a word (the command reads as off), a pick that is no known word (it reads as `commit`), a decision without an outcome, an interval that is a number, a waits-for line that is a list, a description that is a number, a pace pick whose interval is a plain word, a recorded command without its name; output that is no object gives no commands.
- **A pace pick** - a scheduled command carries the pace pick the state holds for it, an interval with a time of day for one command and "whenever there is work" for another; a pace pick held for a command the schedule does not list is not shown.
- **A skill whose `schedule` the tick could not read** - a decision `unreadable schedule: <reason>` under a skill's name makes the project's row name that skill with the reason, and it is no scheduled command's decision.
- **Reading through the command** - `status` runs once in every project; a project whose command fails is a project's row that says why, holds nothing else (no command, no skill named), and leads with "not readable".
- **The cushion in force** - the loosest of the projects' rows' cushions; none when no project's row holds one or there is none.
- **Projects holding another cushion** - of four projects, two at the loosest, one lower and one that answered none, only the lower one is named, its cushion to one decimal; none when all agree or none answered.
- **A typed cushion** - a fraction is rounded, a number out of range is held to −50 or 50, zero is zero; a minus sign alone, a blank and an empty text are no number.
- **Saving the cushion** - `offset -- <points>` runs in every project, a negative fraction after the `--`; with one project failing the answer names it and why.
- **The leading status of a project's row** - "on" in green, "on, not running" when the process is gone, "off" when the scheduler is off.
- **The pace, as a sentence** - an interval alone is "Every 1 day"; a check alone is "When its check finds work", or the skill's plain line when it gives one ("When the queue holds a task"); both are "Every 6 hours at most, when its check finds work", or "Every 15 minutes at most, when a ticket has no plan" with the skill's plain line. An interval spelled out: `1m` is "1 minute", `1h` "1 hour", `7d` "7 days", `2w` "2 weeks", `1mo` "1 month", and `06h` "6 hours"; a plain word, `0d`, `d` and `-3h` are shown as written, and a skill's interval the tool would not have read (`2y`) reads "Every 2y".
- **The pace in force, as a sentence** - for a command whose skill says 6 hours and gives a check: a pace pick of 30 minutes reads "Every 30 minutes at most, when a ticket has no plan", one of 2 days at 10:00 "Every 2 days from 10:00, when a ticket has no plan", one of 2 weeks "Every 2 weeks at most, when a ticket has no plan", and "whenever there is work" "When a ticket has no plan"; for a command with no check, a pace pick of 1 month at 9:05 reads "Every 1 month at 09:05", and "whenever there is work" leaves the skill's "Every 1 day". A pace pick of an interval, and "whenever there is work" for a command with an interval and a check, count as a person's own pace; no pace pick, and "whenever there is work" for a command with no check, do not.
- **The Edit box's draft** - a command with no pace pick opens on "as the skill says"; one with "whenever there is work" opens on that, and on "as the skill says" when its skill gives a check and no interval; one with 2 days at 10:00 opens on "every" with 2, days and 10:00; one with 30 minutes on "every" with 30, minutes and no time. As the words of the command: `skill`, `work`, `2d 10:00`, `15m` with spaces around the count ignored, `1mo`; a time left beside hours is not sent (`6h`); an empty count, `0`, `1.5`, `-2`, a word, `10000` and `1e2` are no pace yet, and the person is told "Type a whole number, from 1 to 9999."; a time of `25:00` is no pace yet; a time field left half typed beside days is no pace yet, and the person is told "Finish the time, like 10:00, or clear it.", while beside hours it does not count (`2h`); a draft that is a pace, and "as the skill says", have nothing to tell. A saved "whenever there is work" that is false beside an interval of 2 days opens on "every" with 2 and days. The command as it would read with a draft saved: "Every 2 days from 10:00, when its check finds work"; the skill's "Every 6 hours at most, when its check finds work" for "as the skill says" over a pace pick; "When its check finds work" for "whenever there is work"; and the command as it is for a draft that is no pace yet.
- **A coming time** - at 21:30 on a Thursday: the same day reads "today 23:00", the next "tomorrow 10:00", two days on "Saturday 09:05", six days on "Wednesday 10:00", seven days on "15 Oct 10:00", and a month on "7 Nov 10:00"; a time earlier the same day, and one five days back, read "as soon as the scheduler looks"; a command switched on here whose last tick said `not due (next start from 2026-10-10 10:00, every 2d at 10:00)` reads "Next: Saturday 10:00", and "Off" when it is switched off here.
- **How many agents at once** - a command with no number is 1; with its skill's 2 it is 2; an agents pick of 1 beats the skill's 2, and an agents pick of 3 holds where the skill says none; in words "One at a time", "Up to 2 at once", "Up to 10 at once". The Edit box's draft: a command with no agents pick opens on "as the skill says", one with 3 on "own" with 3; as the words of the command, `skill`, and `3` for a count typed with spaces and a leading zero; an empty count, `0`, `1.5`, `-2`, a word and `1e2` are no number yet. The command as it would read with a draft saved: 5 over a skill's 2; the skill's 2 again for "as the skill says" over an agents pick of 5; and the command as it is for a draft that is no number yet.
- **A command's numbers of agents** - a scheduled command carries its skill's number when the tick recorded more than one, and the agents pick the state holds for it; a skill's 1 is carried as no number, an agents pick of 0 as none, and an agents pick held for a command the schedule does not list is not shown.
- **How far a command publishes, in words** - by this machine's pick: nothing is "Publishes nothing", `commit` "Commits its work", `branch` "Publishes its branch", `pr` "Opens a pull request", `merge` "Opens a pull request that merges on green".
- **What the scheduler last decided, for a person** - for a command switched on here: `not due` is "No work"; `started <id>` is "Started a run"; `not due (last start 2h ago, every 6h)` is "Started 2h ago, not due yet", and with `less than a minute` "Started less than a minute ago, not due yet"; `cap reached (1 in flight: …)` is "One is already running" and with 3 in flight "3 are already running"; `quota: …`, `check failed: …` and `not ready: …` are the tool's own words with a capital; a last tick that still said `switched off on this machine`, and no decision at all, say nothing. A command switched off here is "Off", whatever the last tick said and with no decision too. A command whose skill is only under `.agents/skills` says "Cannot start: its skill is only in .agents/skills, which Claude Code does not read", switched on or off.
- **The publish menu's picks** - all five with a git host package, "Nothing", "Commit" and "Publish branch" without; a pull request pick in force that the project is not offered is still listed.
