What the tests cover: the "Usage" card's week bar, its budget handle, and the wording it falls back to when there is no reading.

- **Before there is a reading**, the card says it is reading rather than drawing an empty bar, since an empty bar would read as "nothing used".
- **The bar** is described as the percent of the week used against the quota boundary's percent and which day of seven it is; each calendar day gets its own label and delimiter, sized by how much of that day actually falls in the week, with a mid-day start's split day labelled only once, at whichever end most of it falls.
- **One bar only** — the session window and a model's own week never get bars of their own; they are reachable through the bar's "show all limits" tooltip, which lists every window Claude Code reports, in report order, aligned in columns. The tooltip is absent when the account's week is the only window.
- **The budget handle** rests exactly on the quota boundary mark, moves the drawn limit immediately rather than waiting for the next poll, and stores the user's offset from the quota boundary. Successive moves accumulate instead of snapping back to the last polled value. Dragging past either end of the bar still clamps the stored offset to plus or minus fifty points.
- **The two-part fill**: the used share, then the room left before unattended work stops at reduced opacity, drawn as one continuous bar rather than two separate shapes; the second part is absent entirely once the limit sits below what has already been used.
- **Unattended work's state in words**: "enabled" when the handle leaves room to spend, "disabled" when it does not, each with the hint for which way to move the handle and a tooltip explaining what that state means for queued tasks. An "Eager consumption" warning appears only once the handle clears a full day's worth of pace above the quota boundary, and its tooltip states the handle's real deviation ("1 day", "2 days") rather than a fixed figure. The warning and the state share one row with the legend, the warning ahead of the state.
- **The figures under the bar**: the pace deviation as a duration labelled over- or under-consuming (zero exactly on pace), coloured to match the bar; the amount spent as quota time; that amount as a percentage of the pace allowed so far, omitted at the very start of the week when no allowance has elapsed; and the reset as a weekday and time. Each carries its own explanatory tooltip.
- **The legend** names the budget segment and the quota boundary, the latter explaining that it is a pro-rated share of the weekly limit drawn at the current time, not a hard limit.
- **When the week cannot be placed**, three distinct messages: no week line at all, naming both the line expected and the labels that arrived instead; a week with a percentage but no reset time, which explicitly does not claim the week is missing; and a reset phrasing the version does not recognize, quoting it verbatim. In every such case no bar and no handle are drawn, while the windows that were reported still list.
- **When usage is unavailable**: Claude Code not found is explained instead of showing a zeroed bar; an unrecognized readout with earlier numbers retained keeps the bar and dates it as stale; an unrecognized readout with nothing retained says it will try again rather than reading as terminal.
