The site's four pages and what every page shares. The landing page (`/`) makes the pitch and hands out the command that starts The Framework; "Go to dashboard" (`/go-to-dashboard`) tells a visitor looking for the dashboard that it runs on their own machine and how to start it; the press page (`/press`) is the press kit; and the banner page (`/banner`) is the composition that the link-preview image is a screenshot of. Two things cross pages and are described below: the package manager in which every command is shown, and the link-preview image.

## Context

**User story**: a visitor picks "pnpm" once in the hero and every command on the site, today and on their next visit, is a pnpm command. A visitor shares any page of the site and the card that unfurls shows The Framework's banner.

## Business logic — TL;DR

- **Site-wide settings** (`+config.ts`) - every page is pre-rendered to static HTML; the default title "The Framework", the description and the favicon.
- **Head content of every page** (`+Head.tsx`) - restores the package manager choice before first paint, declares the link-preview metadata, loads the typefaces.
- **The landing page** (`index/`) - the pitch, the chapters, the install commands, and the navigation, footer and building blocks every page reuses.
- **The "Go to dashboard" page** (`go-to-dashboard/`) - the three commands that open the dashboard, since a public page cannot open a local one.
- **The press page** (`press/`) - logo, name, banner and brand sources, reached by right-clicking the logo.
- **The banner page** (`banner/`) - the 1200 by 630 pixel composition screenshotted into the link-preview image.
- **The package manager choice** - one choice of `npm`, `pnpm`, `bun` or `yarn` for every command on the site, made with tabs in the hero or on the "Go to dashboard" page, remembered across visits and applied before first paint.
- **The link-preview image** - a hand-made screenshot of the banner page that every page declares as its preview, and that the press page offers and explains how to regenerate.

## Business logic

### The package manager choice

#### Context

See `## Context`.

#### Business logic

The site shows every command — the one-shot run and the global install — in one of four package managers, `npm`, `pnpm`, `bun` or `yarn`, and keeps a single choice for the whole site:

- The choice is made by clicking a tab in any tab row: the "Try:" box of the landing page's hero (`index/Hero.tsx`) or either of the two rows on the "Go to dashboard" page (`go-to-dashboard/+Page.tsx`). All rows and all commands on the page switch at once.
- The choice is saved in the browser's local storage and applied again on every later visit, before the page first renders (`+Head.tsx`), so that a returning visitor never sees another package manager's command flash first.
- Every command is present in the page in all four variants and only the chosen variant is visible (the rule lives in `index/styles.css`). Without JavaScript, npm's variant shows.
- `npm` is the default: no saved choice, an unrecognized saved value, or a browser that refuses access to its storage all mean `npm`. When the browser refuses to save, a click still switches the current page but is forgotten on the next visit.
- What a click on a command copies is the variant visible at that moment. The commands themselves, and why yarn's install line is an npm command, are in `index/Hero.tsx`.

### The link-preview image

#### Context

See `## Context`.

#### Business logic

The image that chats and social networks show for a link to the site is `https://the-framework.ai/banner.jpg`, served from `public/banner.jpg`, and every page declares it together with the site's root URL as the shared address, so that any page of the site unfurls as the home page with the banner (`+Head.tsx`). The image is not generated by the build: it is a screenshot, made by hand, of the banner page (`banner/+Page.tsx`), a 1200 by 630 pixel composition of the pitch with no site navigation. The press page (`press/+Page.tsx`) shows the image, offers it for download, states that it is a screenshot of `/banner`, and links to the instructions for regenerating it, which is what must happen whenever the banner page changes.
