The landing page at `/` — the website's home page and its whole sales pitch, plus the header and footer that the other pages reuse.

The page is one argument told in order: today's coding agents need babysitting, The Framework makes them autonomous instead, here is the machinery that does it, here is what else it can do, and it is yours and not ours — closing with an invitation to join the community.

## Business logic — TL;DR

- **Hero** - the promise and the way in: "Babysit AI" struck through in favour of "Autonomous AI", the three claims (open source, free, local), and the one command that runs the product without installing it.
- **Section navigation** - a sticky table of contents that follows the reader through the five sections below.
- **Stop babysitting** - the problem: five ways an unattended coding agent disappoints, each pairing the bad fix people reach for with what The Framework does instead.
- **Autonomous AI** - the promise: what the product does unasked, split into autonomous product management and autonomous coding, tempered by the reminder that agents still ask before non-obvious decisions.
- **How it works** - the mechanism: the two building blocks — an enhanced system prompt that makes the agent work properly, and the two queues (tasks for the AI, reviews for the human) — powered by open source prompt packs.
- **Features** - the breadth: the user's own AI subscription, quota spent rather than wasted, custom prompts, the dashboard, notifications, cloud and multi-machine execution, and a Discord bot.
- **Your framework** - the trust argument: flexible, 100% local, 100% open source.
- **Call to action** - join the Discord community, star the repository.
